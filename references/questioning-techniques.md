# Questioning Techniques

Use one question at a time. Choose the smallest question that reveals or repairs the current gap.

## The fairness check

Before asking a question, verify:
- Has the learner already been given the information needed?
- Have unfamiliar terms in the question been explained?
- Is the question about the current learning step, rather than a later advanced idea?
- Does it invite thinking rather than guess what the tutor has in mind?

If not, teach the missing piece first.

Do not use a question to smuggle in a new concept. Do not begin a difficult subject with a paradox or a technical puzzle before establishing its foundations.

## Elicit the core idea after teaching

- "How would you explain the idea we just covered in your own words?"
- "What do you think is the main point?"
- "Can you explain it to someone who has never encountered it?"

## Reveal mechanisms and relationships

- "Why does that happen, based on what we've discussed?"
- "How does step A lead to step B?"
- "What would change if this part were removed?"

## Check examples and boundaries

- "Can you give an example from everyday life?"
- "Would this situation count? Why or why not?"
- "Where might this explanation stop being useful?"

## Distinguish nearby concepts

- "How is this different from the idea we just learned?"
- "What do the two ideas share, and where do they diverge?"

## Support without taking over

- Start with a hint tied to something already taught.
- If the learner remains stuck, change approach: use a more familiar example, break the idea into smaller steps, or explain directly.
- Offer a choice only when it reduces cognitive load, not as a substitute for teaching.
- Then invite the learner to restate the idea.

## Check transfer

- "How would you use this idea in a new situation?"
- "What would you predict if one condition changed?"
- "Can you explain the same idea without using the key technical term?"

Avoid leading questions that reveal the answer, repeated interrogation, multiple questions in one turn, and questions unrelated to the current learning goal.
