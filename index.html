A practice in clear thinking

Understanding begins when you can explain it.

A good explanation is more than a neat arrangement of words. It is a way to find out what you understand, what you only recognise, and what you have yet to discover. Feynman helps an AI assistant guide that process.

Two ways in / one method

Begin with a question.
Or bring what you're reading.

Learning can start with a subject you are curious about or with material already on your desk. Both approaches lead into the same cycle of explaining, testing, and rebuilding understanding.

01 / Start with curiosity

Learn a concept

Ask about quantum physics, opportunity cost, the structure of a cell, or an idea you have never quite understood. The assistant begins with a clear explanation, introduces unfamiliar terms, and uses examples before inviting you to explain it back.

02 / Start with a source

Learn from your material

Bring a textbook, PDF, article, webpage, lecture transcript, notes, photograph, or diagram. The assistant works from accessible material, preserves its meaning, and returns to relevant passages when repairing gaps.

The learning cycle / 02

Make the idea simple.
Then make it yours.

The aim is not to memorise a polished answer. It is to build an explanation you can recreate, question, and use independently.

01↘

Understand

Start with a simple, accurate explanation of the central idea.

02↗

Teach it back

Explain the idea in your own words, without leaning on the original phrasing.

03?

Find the gaps

Notice jargon, vague wording, missing steps, and assumptions you cannot defend.

04⌁

Rebuild

Return to one important gap and work through the missing idea carefully.

05↻

Explain again

Reconstruct the explanation independently, with greater clarity and precision.

06→

Apply

Use the idea in a new example and recognise where its limits begin.

A principle to keep / 03

Recognition is not understanding. Your explanation is the evidence.

You may recognise a term, remember a definition, or follow an explanation while it is in front of you. The deeper test is whether you can reconstruct the idea in your own words—and notice what still does not make sense.
A useful question to return to: “Could I explain this without borrowing the sentence I just read?”
An open learning tool / 04

Bring the method
to your AI assistant.

Feynman – The Learning Companion is an Agent Skill, not a standalone app. It is a portable set of instructions and supporting examples that compatible AI assistants can follow. It does not include its own AI model, chat interface, file-processing service, or learner database.

Reading PDFs, browsing webpages, transcribing videos, and interpreting images depend on the tools available in the AI assistant where you install it. The skill is designed to be transparent when a source cannot be accessed.

A few clarifications / 05

Questions, before you begin.

A little about the method, the material it can work with, and what this project actually provides.

What is the Feynman Technique?

It is a learning approach built around explaining an idea in simple language, noticing where the explanation breaks down, returning to the missing knowledge, and explaining it again. The aim is understanding, not merely repeating a definition.

Does the assistant explain before asking questions?

Yes. For a normal learning request, it first gives a beginner-friendly explanation and then invites the learner to teach it back. It can test first when the learner explicitly requests that.

Can I study from a PDF, book, webpage, video, or image?

The skill supports learning from these materials when the host AI agent can access and interpret them. Video transcription, PDF extraction, webpage browsing, and image reading are capabilities of the host agent, not built into this instruction package.

Is Feynman Learning Companion a standalone app?

No. It is a portable set of instructions for compatible AI agents. It does not include its own AI model, chat interface, file-processing service, or persistent learner database.

Where can I get the skill?

The complete skill is available in the public GitHub repository. Download the repository and follow its installation instructions.
